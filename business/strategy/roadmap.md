# GyroAuth Business Roadmap — Initial Six Months

## Principle

The roadmap is evidence-driven. Progress is measured by whether GyroAuth becomes easier to understand, test, evaluate, and adopt—not by the amount of business documentation produced.

## Month 1 — Entry Materials

- finalize audience-specific one-sentence explanations;
- reduce the initial customer problems to three;
- rank initial use cases;
- prepare a company-facing one-page technical overview;
- draft the primary PoC proposal and FAQ;
- define a clear inquiry/contact path from the public demo;
- understand AUBA participation and publication requirements;
- publish or prepare ResearchHub materials;
- align ORCID and public research profiles.

## Month 2 — Comparison and Evaluation Design

- compare GyroAuth with continuous authentication, risk-based authentication, UEBA, and Zero Trust controls;
- define PoC metrics and acceptance criteria;
- define required log fields and normalization rules;
- design offline and Shadow Mode architectures;
- define CSV and JSON schemas;
- add at least one use-case-specific synthetic scenario;
- conduct security, privacy, and operational-risk reviews.

## Month 3 — External Discovery

Candidate contacts:

- authentication and security vendors;
- security SIers;
- university laboratories;
- manufacturing and OT security organizations;
- AI-agent platform providers;
- public programs, grants, and accelerators;
- AUBA participants.

Initial activity targets:

- five problem-discovery interviews;
- three technical explanations or demonstrations;
- at least one plausible PoC candidate.

## Month 4 — Limited PoC

Preferred sequence:

1. offline evaluation using synthetic, anonymized, or customer-provided logs;
2. comparison with existing rules or decisions;
3. operator review of mismatches and FREEZE/REVIEW events;
4. result report with false-positive candidates, missed-event candidates, and operational burden.

The PoC should not initially block production access.

## Month 5 — Improvement and Publishable Evidence

- separate confidential and publishable PoC findings;
- improve the demo and technical materials;
- update the FAQ and competitive comparison;
- estimate implementation and operating costs;
- document functions required for productionization;
- consider a comparison paper or technical report.

## Month 6 — Continuation Decision

Evaluate the following paths:

- paid PoC continuation;
- collaborative research;
- technical licensing;
- contract development or consulting;
- security-vendor integration;
- grant application;
- MVP productization;
- change of initial market;
- research-first continuation.

## Go / No-Go Evidence

A business path should continue only when there is evidence of at least one of the following:

- a customer problem that existing controls do not address adequately;
- access to usable evaluation data;
- a stakeholder willing to spend time on a structured PoC;
- a measurable advantage or operational distinction;
- a realistic delivery model for a small team.

A path should be paused or narrowed when:

- required data cannot be obtained;
- the value is explainable only through Gyro-specific terminology;
- existing products solve the problem sufficiently;
- validation requires production liability that cannot be accepted;
- PoC operation requires disproportionate manual review;
- no transition from PoC to paid work can be articulated.
